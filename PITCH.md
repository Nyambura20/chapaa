# Chapaa — pitch slides

One slide per section. The line under **Say** is for the speaker, not for the slide.

---

## 1. Title

**Chapaa**
Keeps the money. Keeps the line.

For people in Kenya. Built on Africa's Talking.

**Say:** Chapaa does two things before someone loses money. It checks the message. It checks the face.

---

## 2. The thirty seconds that cost a term's fees

A parent gets this:

> Dear Parent, pay KES 14,500 Term 3 fees to Paybill 522123 Acc 0178 MARANDA immediately or the student is sent home.

The Paybill is not the school's. The panic is the product.

The second attack is quieter. Someone swaps the SIM, resets M-Pesa, and the line is gone before the owner reaches a shop.

**Say:** School-fee SMS and SIM swap are the two losses we stop people walking into.

---

## 3. What people have today

- The SMS looks official.
- The Paybill is a number, not a name they can trust.
- A SIM change can happen with a story at a shop, not with the owner's face.
- The warning, when it comes, comes after the money has moved.

**Say:** The dangerous moment is the ten seconds before they press send.

---

## 4. Chapaa in one line

**Check the message before you pay. Check the face before the line changes.**

English and Kiswahili. Big type. A **Sikia** button when they want it read aloud. Nothing speaks until they press it.

**Say:** If they do not press Sikia, the page stays quiet.

---

## 5. Two doors

| Door | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| **Angalia ujumbe** | Paste the SMS, or read it into the microphone |
| **Badilisha laini** | Prove the face matches the ID |

Staff open **Ofisi** and see every check that just happened.

**Say:** The home screen is two buttons. That is the whole product.

---

## 6. Message check — what the person does

1. Paste the SMS, or press **Soma kwa sauti** and read it.
2. The words land in the box. They can fix them.
3. They choose their county.
4. They type their number, starting with `+254`.
5. They choose **Ujumbe** or **Simu**.

**Say:** The microphone writes into the box. It does not score the message by itself. They still press continue.

---

## 7. How a message is judged

Three answers only:

- **Usitume pesa** — likely a scam
- **Uliza wenyewe** — suspicious
- **Bado uliza** — no red flags, still confirm with the real person

Rules run first. A second reader can only raise the verdict. It cannot call a dangerous message safe.

**Say:** We would rather say "ask them" than tell someone a scam is fine.

---

## 8. What makes a message a scam

Caught on the text itself:

- It asks for a PIN, OTP, or secret code
- The Paybill, Till, phone, or link was already reported
- A prize that wants money or a link
- "Your account will be blocked" plus a link or a PIN
- A fee before a loan is sent
- "Reverse this money now"

Then the Paybill is checked against known fraudulent numbers and against real schools and licensed lenders.

**Say:** Paybill 522123 on the Maranda fee SMS is the demo. It is not the school's number.

---

## 9. The warning leaves on their phone

Africa's Talking sends the answer.

- **Message** — an SMS on the number they typed
- **Call** — a voice warning they can answer

**Sikia** on the screen reads the same result, only if they press it.

**Say:** Show the Maranda SMS, pick a county, choose Message, and let the phone buzz.

---

## 10. The line check

**Badilisha laini** is an identity check, not a button that swaps a SIM.

1. They type the number and allow the camera.
2. Africa's Talking says whether that line was swapped recently.
3. A recent swap stops here. The network is not asked to change the SIM.
4. If the line is clear: photo of the ID, photo of the live face, a 4-digit number they hear and type, then a confirmation code.

Photos are not saved. Only the match result is stored.

**Say:** Say this out loud so nobody thinks we replace Safaricom. We record the check. The telco still owns the swap.

---

## 11. What "matched" means

- The face on the ID and the live face are compared.
- The 4-digit number is the proof they are present, not a photo held up to the camera.
- Pass, and the check is marked complete.
- Fail, and they start again. No line is changed.

**Say:** A number ending in 999 is the demo for "this SIM was changed recently."

## 12. Ofisi — the staff room

The same checks, from the other side.

- **Dashibodi** — messages checked, how many looked like scams, how many face checks passed
- **Vitisho** — every message, the score, and the reason
- **Mtandao** — a map of Kenya
- **Badilisha laini** — the same face check, for a demo at the table
- **Mipangilio** — Africa's Talking keys and callback addresses

**Say:** Open Ofisi after the live SMS so the judges see the row appear.

---

## 13. The map is where the scams are

**Mtandao** is not a signal map.

Each pin is a county where people checked a scam or a suspicious message.

- One report — small amber pin
- Two to four — larger red pin
- Five or more — the largest, darkest pin

The list beside the map shows the count and the latest Paybill or phone. Clear messages are not plotted.

Counties on the check: Nairobi, Mombasa, Kisumu, Nakuru, Uasin Gishu, Kiambu, Machakos, Kakamega, Nyeri, Meru, Kisii, Garissa.

**Say:** After the demo SMS, the county they picked should be on this map.

---

## 14. What is wired to Africa's Talking

- Outbound SMS warning
- Outbound voice warning
- SIM-swap status lookup before the face check
- Incoming SMS webhook and voice callback, when the public URL is set

Shortcode on the staff rail: **20880**.

**Say:** If the key is loaded, health says live. If it is empty, the score still runs on this machine and the screen says so.

## 15. Three-minute demo

1. Home. Press **Sikia** once, so they hear the line. Then stop talking.
2. **Angalia ujumbe.** Paste the Maranda fee SMS. Choose Nairobi. Send by message.
3. Read the verdict: do not pay. Show the reason: the Paybill.
4. **Badilisha laini.** Use a number ending in 999 and show the block. Then a normal number, ID, face, the 4 digits, the code.
5. **Ofisi → Mtandao.** Nairobi is on the map because of the message just checked.

**Say:** Do not open the device list. The proof is the SMS, the face, and the map.

## 16. Close

Someone in Kenya should be able to ask one question before they send money:

**Is this message real, and is this still my line?**

Chapaa answers both, in the language they pressed, on the phone they already have.

**Say:** Stop. Let the last line sit. Then take questions.

---

## If they ask

**Does Chapaa swap the SIM?**
No. It records the identity check. The mobile network is not asked to swap the SIM.

**Can the model mark a scam as safe?**
No. Rules run first. The second reader can only make the verdict stricter.

**Does the page talk by itself?**
No. **Sikia** speaks only when pressed.

**Where do the map pins come from?**
From message checks where the person chose a county and the verdict was scam or suspicious.

**What about the device cards under Vifaa?**
They are sample handsets. They are not the demo.
